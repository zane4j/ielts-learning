03 / Band 6.5+ 参考修订稿

四自然段，五色背景标记；基于此前保存的练习笔记参考稿，非用户原文

The line graph compares the percentages of commuters travelling to work by car, public transport and bicycle in a city from 2000 to 2020.

Overall, car use declined steadily, while public transport became increasingly popular and overtook cars by 2015. Cycling remained the least common means of commuting throughout the period, changing only slightly.

In 2000, cars were by far the most popular option, accounting for 60% of commuters, compared with 25% for public transport. The share of car users then fell to 48% in 2010 and 42% in 2015. By contrast, public transport use rose to 37% and 43% in these years, respectively, before reaching 48% in 2020, when car use had dropped to 35%.

Bicycle use showed much less change. It accounted for 15% of commuters in 2000 and remained at this level until 2015. By 2020, the figure had edged up to 17%, still well below the proportions using cars and public transport. At the end of the period, cycling was less than half as common as travelling by car.

04 / Band 7.5 目标范文

重新编写的学习目标范文，非官方评分结果。

The line chart illustrates changes in the proportion of people commuting by three forms of transport in a city between 2000 and 2020.

Overall, the proportion of car commuters fell considerably, whereas public transport use rose to become the leading mode of travel. Cycling remained the least popular option and showed only a marginal increase.

At the beginning, 60% of commuters travelled by car, more than twice the figure for public transport, at 25%. Car use decreased steadily to 48% in 2010 and 42% in 2015. By comparison, the share taking public transport climbed from 25% to 37% in 2010 and then 43% in 2015, overtaking driving for the first time.

By 2020, public transport accounted for 48% of commuters, while the car figure had dropped to 35%. Cycling, meanwhile, was relatively stable at 15% from 2000 to 2015, before increasing slightly to 17% at the end. This final share was substantially lower than those of both other methods.
