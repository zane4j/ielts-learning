## Question

> Some students take a year off between finishing school and starting university to work or travel. Why do they do this? What effects can this have on their studies?

## Sample Essay

Some school leavers decide to work or travel for a year before beginning university. They often do this to save money or gain independence. A gap year can improve their motivation and practical skills, although it may also affect their study habits if the time is used without clear goals.

One common reason for taking a gap year is the cost of higher education. Tuition fees, rent and everyday expenses can place considerable pressure on students and their families. By working for several months, young adults may save money and learn how to manage a budget. Other students choose to travel because they want to experience different cultures and become more independent before committing to a degree. Some also need additional time to decide which subject best suits their interests and career ambitions. These reasons show that a gap year is often a practical choice rather than simply an extended holiday.

The effect on university studies depends on how students spend this period. Employment can teach time management, teamwork and communication, while travel can improve adaptability and confidence. These experiences may help students understand their goals and participate more actively in university life. However, a year without regular academic work can also create difficulties. Some students may forget important knowledge or struggle to return to a structured study routine. Reading regularly, taking a short course or reviewing essential subjects could reduce these risks. Therefore, the most valuable gap years are those that combine personal experience with a realistic plan for returning to education.

In conclusion, students usually take a gap year to improve their financial situation or gain life experience. When used productively, it can strengthen their readiness for university, but an unplanned break may make studying more difficult.
