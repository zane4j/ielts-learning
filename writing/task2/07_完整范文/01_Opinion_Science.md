## Question

> The most important aim of science should be to improve people's lives. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

## Sample Essay

Scientific research influences almost every aspect of modern life. Many people argue that its most important goal should be to improve human well-being. I largely agree because research can address urgent social needs, although I also believe that basic science without immediate practical applications deserves financial support.

First, scientific progress can directly protect people's health. Many families still face serious illnesses, and some communities have limited access to reliable medical services. When researchers develop vaccines, diagnostic tools and more effective treatments, doctors can prevent disease or identify problems earlier. These developments can reduce suffering and help patients return to their normal lives. For example, improved testing technology may allow doctors in smaller hospitals to detect conditions that previously required specialist facilities. Governments therefore have good reasons to prioritise medical and public-health research, since its benefits are both practical and widely shared.

However, scientific value cannot always be measured by immediate results. Some researchers study basic physical or biological questions without knowing whether their findings will have commercial applications. In the future, this knowledge may become essential to technologies that nobody originally imagined. If public funding were limited to projects promising quick improvements, important discoveries could be lost. Universities and research institutions should therefore maintain support for long-term studies while directing substantial resources towards current human problems. This balanced policy recognises both the social responsibility of science and the uncertainty involved in genuine discovery.

In conclusion, improving people's lives should be a central priority of scientific research because it addresses urgent needs. Nevertheless, basic science remains important, and it should not be abandoned simply because its benefits are difficult to predict.
