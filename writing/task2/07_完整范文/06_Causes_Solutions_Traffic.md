## Question

> Traffic congestion is a serious problem in many cities. What are the main causes, and what measures can be taken to address it?

## Sample Essay

Traffic congestion is a growing problem in many urban areas, making journeys slower and increasing air pollution. Its main causes are heavy dependence on private cars and the distance between homes and workplaces. Improving public transport and changing urban planning are effective ways to address it.

One major cause is that public transport is often less convenient than driving. In some neighbourhoods, buses are infrequent, train stations are far away and different services are poorly connected. As a result, people choose to travel by car, even when they are making relatively short journeys. In addition, new housing areas may be built far from employment centres, so large numbers of workers must commute every day. During rush hour, these vehicles enter the same roads at similar times and exceed the available capacity. Building more roads may provide temporary relief, but it does not remove the reasons why residents rely on their cars.

The most useful solution is to make public transport a realistic alternative. More frequent buses, reliable rail services and affordable tickets could persuade commuters to leave their cars at home. Transport authorities should also improve the links between residential areas and major stations, reducing unnecessary transfers. Over the longer term, city planners can develop neighbourhoods with housing, schools, shops and workplaces located closer together. This would reduce the number and length of journeys people need to make. Combining improved transport with better land-use planning would therefore address both the immediate problem of car dependence and the underlying causes of long commutes.

In conclusion, traffic congestion is largely caused by inadequate public transport and urban areas that require long car journeys. A combination of reliable transport services and more compact city planning can reduce the problem.
