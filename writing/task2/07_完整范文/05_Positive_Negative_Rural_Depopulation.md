## Question

> In many countries, people are moving from rural areas to cities, so the population in the countryside is decreasing. Is this a positive or negative development?

## Sample Essay

Many rural residents are moving to cities because they expect better employment and education opportunities. Although this decision may improve individual lives, I consider the continued decline of countryside populations a largely negative development because it weakens local communities and increases pressure on urban services.

One important problem is that shrinking rural populations can damage essential services. When younger residents leave, local schools, shops and bus services may lose the customers or funding they need to operate. Older people who remain in these communities can then find it harder to buy necessities or receive medical treatment. Local businesses may also struggle to recruit workers, and some agricultural activities become less sustainable. These problems can create a cycle: as services disappear, even more people feel they must move elsewhere. Consequently, rural depopulation can reduce the quality of life for those who choose or need to remain in their hometowns.

A second disadvantage is the pressure on growing cities. When large numbers of newcomers arrive, housing, roads and hospitals may not expand quickly enough. This can lead to higher rents, crowded public transport and longer commuting times. Admittedly, moving to a city can provide higher wages and greater career choices. However, those private benefits do not eliminate the wider costs of an unbalanced population distribution. Governments could ease the problem by supporting regional businesses, improving public services and investing in reliable transport outside major cities. Such policies would help people remain in smaller communities without sacrificing opportunities.

In conclusion, rural-to-urban migration can benefit individuals, but widespread rural depopulation creates long-term difficulties in both the countryside and cities. Overall, I regard it as a negative trend.
