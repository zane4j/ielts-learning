## Question

> Some university students want to learn about other subjects in addition to their main subjects. Others believe it is more important to give all their time and attention to studying for a qualification. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

## Sample Essay

University students often disagree about how much time to spend on courses outside their major. Some believe that broader study makes graduates more capable, while others argue that students should concentrate on obtaining a strong qualification. In my view, the main subject should remain the priority, but selected additional courses can also be valuable.

Supporters of specialised education point out that many degree programmes already require considerable effort. Engineering and medical students, for example, must understand complex theories and complete demanding practical work. If they spend too much time on unrelated classes, they may struggle to master knowledge needed in their future careers. Employers also expect graduates to demonstrate expertise in specific areas rather than only a general understanding of several subjects. Consequently, focusing on a major can help students achieve better examination results and become more confident in their professional abilities.

On the other hand, learning about different subjects can develop skills that specialist courses do not always provide. Modern workplaces frequently require collaboration across departments, and employees benefit from understanding the needs of colleagues and customers. For instance, a computer science student who takes a business or communication course may become more effective at explaining technical decisions. Studying a wider range of topics can also help young adults explore interests and make more informed career choices. However, these benefits are greatest when optional classes complement the main qualification rather than replace essential coursework.

In conclusion, strong specialist knowledge is important for academic and professional success. Nevertheless, I believe students should be allowed to take a limited number of additional subjects because these can support broader career development.
